Final artifact: proposal.py, with deterministic propose(case, assignment).

Assay and objective
The case describes the AMFR human 47-residue fragment, assayed for stability by cDNA-display proteolysis (AMFR_HUMAN_Tsuboyama_2023_4G3O). Higher supplied DMS_score is the signed ranking target. We do not estimate probabilities, calibrated free energies, or experimental improvement. No measured WT outcome is assumed. Scores are final query-rank percentiles mapped into [-1,1]. No further variants are selected.

Measured feedback
All 96 provided observations (32 original single mutants and 64 independent feedback variants) enter each final fit equally. They include 52 singles and 44 doubles. Feedback supplies information about combinations at sites 28, 32, 38, and 41, and about substitutions not represented in the original single-mutant supports. Every invocation reconstructs features, target transforms, and five regression fits solely from the currently supplied support. There are no copied outcomes, learned coefficients, ID tables, training-data reads, serialized models, or previous-fit states in the predictor. Query fitness fields are never used.

Model
Fixed chemical descriptors cover hydropathy, side-chain volume, helix propensity, approximate charge, aromaticity, proline, glycine, polar uncharged residues, beta branching, and cysteine. Mutation features are differences from the reference. Site indicators and site-specific chemical effects learn positional sensitivity; summed chemical effects provide conservative transfer to sparsely measured or unseen sites. An intercept is fitted rather than fixing WT fitness.

Two whole-sequence Gaussian kernel models combine chemical distances with an additive main-effect kernel. These permit smooth nonadditive combination effects. Two site-additive amino-acid similarity models (one reference-centered) include a small quadratic kernel term. These provide an alternative extrapolation for wholly unseen substitutions. All four models regress training-only empirical ranks with positive ridge regularization. A fifth smooth model uses a monotone inverse-softplus transform of the original readout, offset below the current training minimum by 0.8 assay units. This offset is a validated statistical scale choice, not evidence of a physical assay floor. Its value is recomputed from each training subset.

The ensemble assigns 40% to the alternative target-scale model. The remaining 60% uses the two rank-target sequence models; for queries containing any previously unobserved mutation token, it blends these equally with the two chemical-similarity models. Prediction ranks are averaged to avoid treating the component scales as calibrated. All configurations and weights are fixed validation-selected constants. Final computations are deterministic and need no random fitting step; the documented seed is 1729. Exploration splits used explicit fixed seeds.

Validation and alternatives
Exploration compared exact categorical, physical, amino-acid Gaussian, reference-difference, and fixed BLOSUM similarity kernels; additive and quadratic interactions; whole-sequence Gaussian geometries; raw, rank, and inverse-softplus targets; support-vector regression; and extra trees. Repeated five-fold mixed-support checks were paired with four-fold checks that retained original singles and withheld feedback observations. Separate mutation-token holdouts removed every training record carrying a withheld substitution, testing extrapolation rather than only new pair combinations. Sequence-derived helical-face and local packing hypotheses were examined as transfer features; they did not improve the later diagnostics consistently and were excluded. BLOSUM, trees, and support-vector alternatives were also excluded after comparison.

After freezing the candidate blend, further checks used four new mixed/feedback split seeds and 50 new token holdouts. Mean fold Spearman was approximately 0.758 on mixed supports, 0.810 on withheld feedback, and 0.687 on substitution-token holdouts. Within double mutants, corresponding means were approximately 0.760, 0.764, and 0.670. The adaptive monotone-transform offset performed essentially like the initially tested fixed offset. These numbers are internal diagnostics on the same finite collection of measured supports. Overlapping folds, model selection, small test sets, and differing mutation coverage prevent interpreting them as an unbiased estimate of future assay accuracy. They are not experimental mechanistic evidence.

Remaining limitations
The observations sparsely sample many positions and substitution classes, and most query variants contain an unobserved mutation token. No external sequences, evolutionary alignment, coordinates, pretrained scores, or hidden labels were available. Chemical similarity and kernel interactions are predictive priors, not demonstrated structural mechanisms. The ensemble regularizes extrapolation but cannot resolve unknown position-specific chemistry or interaction patterns without measurements. Assay noise and transformations may affect subtle orderings; absolute output scores and their spacing are deliberately arbitrary ordinal quantities.

Implementation and checks
The executable module is self-contained apart from local NumPy/SciPy dependencies. It reads input only through its arguments, refits on every call, and returns every supplied query ID with a finite score in [-1,1], plus selected_ids=[]. Empty queries and empty supports are handled explicitly. Exploration files are retained. The required protected executor is run after artifact finalization; its output is recorded in trial_output.json.

Protected executor verification completed successfully (exit code 0) using the required command; trial_output.json was generated. Direct contract checks also verified all 186 query IDs, bounds, finite values, repeat-call determinism, empty selection, and refitting with support sizes 1, 16, 32, and 64.
