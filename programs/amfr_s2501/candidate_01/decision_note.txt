Final decision: a deterministic five-member ordinal ensemble. proposal.py is
self-contained and refits all learned parameters from case['support'] on every
call. It returns every current query ID, finite scores in [-1,1], and no selected
measurements. Neither assignment eligibility lists nor stored experimental data
are used to fit the predictor. There are no model caches or prediction-file reads.

Assay interpretation
This is the AMFR human CUE-domain stability assay measured by cDNA-display
proteolysis, with ProteinGym DMS_score as supplied and higher fitness preferred.
The task is signed rank correlation. We neither estimate calibrated assay
probabilities nor claim improved experimental stability. No measured wild-type
score or fixed wild-type fitness is imposed. The ridge members learn a target
intercept from current support ranks. We considered inverse soft-floor transforms
as a possible response to assay saturation; they did not justify replacing the
ordinal models. An assay floor or physical stability mechanism is not established.

How measured feedback is used
All 96 supplied observations, including the 64 independently sampled feedback
variants, enter the final fits. The original 32 measurements alone mainly describe
isolated single mutants. Feedback adds 44 measured double mutants and substantial
coverage at positions 28, 32, 38, and 41. The model learns substitution effects at
these sites jointly rather than assigning unmeasured single-mutant labels or
assuming doubles equal the sum of measured single mutants. The same fitting code
also works with smaller supplied support subsets; absent feedback is never read
from elsewhere or reconstructed from remembered outcomes.

Representations and model
Reference-relative mutation features use fixed hydropathy, residue volume,
alpha-helix propensity, charge, aromatic character, proline/glycine indicators,
polar character, and branching. Position-specific biochemical kernels share
information among related substitutions, while pooled chemical changes transfer
information across sites. Two members additionally use a small contribution from
the generic BLOSUM62 substitution matrix, projected to a positive-semidefinite
kernel. BLOSUM62 is a scientific constant, not a pretrained assay predictor or an
external sequence. One member shares destination amino-acid indicators globally.
Weak site-pair indicator kernels allow nonadditive combination offsets with
shrinkage. These are statistical offsets, not asserted residue contacts.

Three kernel-ridge members fit Gaussianized ranks calculated only from the current
training labels. Two kernel pairwise-logistic members fit the signed ordering of
current measured pairs directly. Their logistic objective is a ranking loss, not
a calibrated probability model. The ensemble combines 70% ridge-family mean
ranks with 30% pairwise-family mean ranks, then maps the combined query ordering
into [-1,1]. This scale has ordinal meaning only. Every call recomputes the chemical
kernels, rank transforms, fitted coefficients, pairwise objectives and predictions.

Validation and choices
Development compared categorical and physicochemical additive kernels, several
shrinkage scales, Gaussian-rank and raw targets, inverse-soft-floor targets,
position-pair and smooth chemical interaction kernels, source-conditioned global
chemical features, BLOSUM and amino-acid classes, extra trees, gradient boosting,
and pairwise ranking losses. Strong shrinkage and chemically shared ordinal
models were more consistent than the tree models or elaborate interaction terms.
A compact ensemble reduces reliance on one chemical distance or one ordinal loss.
No structural coordinates, MSA, external sequences, pretrained prediction scores,
query outcomes, previous evaluation results, or additional measurements were used.

The main development validation retained the original 32 singles and held out
feedback: four seeded four-fold partitions of the 64 feedback records, plus eight
seeded half-feedback holdouts. Final target rank transforms were computed within
each training fold. The leading ridge configurations had mean feedback Spearman
around 0.74 in this development scheme. Confirmation used four further seeded
four-fold feedback partitions; the final 70/30 ensemble had mean Spearman 0.8009.
When arbitrary supports were withheld in five shuffled folds, its mean Spearman
was 0.6255. Pooled double-mutant Spearman across those confirmation protocols was
about 0.6774; fold-relative member ranks make this pooled number descriptive.
These are repeated internal validation estimates on reused public supports, not
independent experimental evidence or an unbiased confidence bound after model
selection. Different partition results also show material small-sample uncertainty.
Exploration scripts and validation result files are retained in the workspace.

Limitations and reproducibility
Unseen alleles and sparsely measured sites require chemical extrapolation. Native
context can change residue preferences in ways these generic descriptors miss.
The sparse double-mutant data do not identify detailed physical epistasis. Helical
packing and hydrophobic stabilization are plausible sequence-derived explanations
for some observed trends, but neither contacts nor mechanisms are established and
no guessed contact map was imposed. Assay saturation, noise and local effects may
change rankings. The final output is a prediction of the unmeasured ordering only.

NumPy seed is fixed at zero; fitting uses deterministic algorithms and no random
draws. BLAS thread pools are limited to one within each fit. Local checks confirm
exact repeated-call determinism, all-query coverage, bounded finite scores, an
empty selected_ids list, and independent refitting on 1, 32 and 64 supports.
