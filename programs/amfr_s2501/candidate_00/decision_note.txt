Decision: deterministic, support-only ordinal kernel ensemble

Assay and objective
The supplied assay is AMFR_HUMAN_Tsuboyama_2023_4G3O, stability measured by cDNA display proteolysis. Higher supplied DMS_score is better. The task is signed rank correlation, not probability calibration, absolute stability estimation, or demonstrated wet-lab improvement. No measured wild-type score is assumed. The intercept is fitted from observations; wild-type anchoring applies to mutation features, not to an invented WT assay label.

Use of observed feedback
All finite support outcomes supplied to propose() are used afresh, including the independently sampled feedback singles and double mutants. The current case has 32 original singles and 64 feedback records (20 singles and 44 doubles). The feedback makes substitution effects and nonlinear combinations at frequently sampled positions learnable. Support IDs and query IDs are never predictive features, and neither measurements nor fitted coefficients are stored in proposal.py. The implementation also works on public support subsets without needing the current 96 outcomes. Assignment carries no prediction information; selected_ids is always empty because no more measurements are available.

Model
Nine regularized kernel regressions combine mutation-specific categorical similarity, position effects, wild-type-anchored physicochemical amino-acid similarity, shared chemical changes, mutation count, and ANOVA cross-position interactions. Additional kernels share effects according to reference-residue chemistry, chemical-change magnitude, pooled products of changes at different mutated positions, and a sequence-derived environment proxy. All kernel weights and noise levels in the code are fixed validation-selected hyperparameters, not learned coefficients. Every invocation recomputes features and solves all support-dependent regressions.

The environment proxy averages reference hydropathy at offsets +/-3 and +/-4. Those offsets reflect a possible alpha-helical face in this short sequence. This is an assumed, sequence-derived predictive annotation, not structural coordinates, evidence of burial, or a mechanism established experimentally. No MSA, external sequences, pretrained predictor, external substitution matrix, structure, hidden outcome, network access, or stored fitted model is used. Chemical constants are documented in the source.

Most ensemble members fit normal quantiles of empirical support fitness ranks. One fits a monotone asinh transform. Predictions are combined in rank space, avoiding incompatible target scales. Five members form the main combination expert; four provide a conservative single-substitution expert. Singles receive 25% main and 75% specialist rank weight. Final scores are bounded empirical ordinal ranks in [-1,1]. Their absolute values do not have a biological or probabilistic interpretation.

Validation and selection
Exploration used deterministic seeds 2026, 91379 and 981. First, repeated four-fold feedback holdouts retained the original 32 supports in training; the development objective emphasized doubles (75% double-mutant Spearman and 25% all-feedback Spearman), reflecting the query set's predominance of doubles. We compared categorical/additive kernels, chemical RBF bandwidths, shrinkage strengths, interactions, response transforms, source-conditional sharing, chemical products, and the environment proxy. ExtraTrees, RandomForest, and gradient boosting were weaker in the tested configurations. Expanding the low end via hypothetical assay floors did not justify selecting a floor model.

A subsequent eight-repeat partition check favored the five-member main ensemble (about 0.785 feedback and 0.806 double-mutant signed Spearman). The chosen single-specialist blend yielded about 0.806 all-feedback Spearman and 0.571 within feedback singles on that check. A six-repeat four-fold holdout of all supports, including the original singles, gave about 0.720 overall, 0.759 within doubles, and 0.402 within singles for the selected blend. These are pooled out-of-fold correlations averaged over repeats, not mean tiny-fold correlations. Grouped holdouts removing all observations of a particular target amino acid at each hotspot gave approximately 0.64, 0.63, 0.73, and 0.84 correlations for positions 28, 32, 38, and 41 respectively with a related ten-member ensemble. This was an extrapolation diagnostic rather than the exact final ensemble estimate.

All checks reuse the same finite observed supports. Hyperparameters and the expert blend were selected using these outcomes, so these validation numbers are selection-influenced and are not independent experimental evidence or an unbiased final generalization estimate. New partitions do not create new experimental observations. Final query fitness remains unobserved.

Limitations
Sparse sites and previously unseen amino acids require strong chemical sharing and shrinkage; single mutants are demonstrably less predictable under full-support holdout. Pair kernels do not establish physical residue contacts or causal epistasis. Assay noise, scale nonlinearities, and uneven coverage may change the ranking. There are no uncertainty-calibrated probabilities or claims that top-ranked variants will improve experimentally. Model configuration transfer to smaller public support subsets remains an extrapolation of the validation-selected priors, but each subset's outcomes alone determine its fitted coefficients.

Artifact checks
proposal.py is self-contained apart from local NumPy and SciPy. It performs no file reads and has no persistent fitted state. Seeds are set on every invocation, and the numerical fits are deterministic. Two current-case calls returned identical results, covered all 186 current queries, and completed together in about 0.21 seconds locally. A separate fit using only the original 32 supports also covered all query IDs. The required protected executor is run after writing this note to verify the final artifact and output contract.
