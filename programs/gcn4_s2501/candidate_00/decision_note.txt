Decision: support-trained nonlinear chemistry ensemble for ordinal GCN4 ranking.

Assay interpretation
The supplied endpoint is Binding, selection is FACS, organism is S. cerevisiae,
and outcomes are ProteinGym DMS_score in original assay units. Larger observed
fitness is the signed ranking target. There is no measured WT; the reference is
used only to locate substitutions and sequence features, never as a fitness
anchor. No probability, absolute effect calibration, or wet-lab improvement is
claimed. All observed values, including the extreme single-mutant outcome, are
retained. Within each fit, observed fitness is converted to normal scores of its
empirical ranks. This preserves ordering and limits extreme numerical leverage.

Use of feedback and sequence hypotheses
Every invocation fits anew using ONLY that invocation's support records. In the
current case this includes 32 single mutants and 64 measured feedback variants.
No outcome or prediction ID is embedded in proposal.py. No file is read by the
predictor; no pretrained weights, external sequences, MSA, or structure are used.

Features describe the assayed residues 101-144: residue composition, counts of
acidic/basic/aromatic/hydrophobic/polar residues, coarse regional counts,
mutation burden, and simple hydropathy/helix-propensity constants. The WxxLF
pattern at 120-124 and its surrounding chemistry are assumed sequence
annotations, not measured structural coordinates. Additional descriptors count
local acidic-hydrophobic proximity and clustering. They express hypotheses
about sequence context and combination effects; their presence does not
establish a molecular mechanism. Correlated, redundant descriptors are handled
with shrinkage or restricted tree leaves.

The final predictor averages five families on the same transformed outcome:
25% regularized radial-basis chemistry kernels (three smoothness/shrinkage
settings), 25% global-composition ExtraTrees, 10% regional-chemistry ExtraTrees,
15% strongly regularized focused chemistry/motif ridge regression, and 25%
shallow robust gradient boosting on the focused descriptors. Each ExtraTrees
family averages leaf sizes 3, 5, and 8 with fixed seeds. This combines smooth
similarity regression, composition effects, and learned nonlinear interactions.
All scaling statistics, target transforms, coefficients, and tree splits are
estimated from the supplied supports. Final query scores are empirical ordinal
ranks mapped strictly inside [-1,1]; ties remain ties. selected_ids is empty.

Validation and model selection
Development compared whole-domain and regional composition, chemistry,
position-specific properties, exact substitution indicators, local context,
linear/radial/polynomial kernels, raw/clipped/log/rank targets, single-mutant
weighting, separate mutation-burden experts, elastic net, PLS, SVR, random
forests, ExtraTrees, and gradient boosting. Six repeated stratified four-fold
splits held out feedback variants while retaining original single mutants.
There are 36 feedback variants with 2-6 substitutions and 28 with 36-44; these
regimes were stratified. Selection favored rank targets, chemistry smoothing,
and conservative trees over detailed mutation-specific coefficients.

Confirmation used four additional four-fold partitions and three two-fold
partitions, always recomputing scaling and outcome transforms inside the
training fold. Four-fold fits contain 80 supports; two-fold fits contain 64.
The final ensemble's mean signed Spearman correlation across all 64 feedback
out-of-fold predictions was 0.434 (SD across partitions 0.030) and 0.400
(SD 0.053), respectively. Corresponding within-regime means were 0.535/0.553
for low mutation burden and 0.142/0.134 for high burden. These SDs describe
split sensitivity, not confidence intervals. Validation artifacts and scripts
are left in the workspace. Configuration selection used these observed data;
these are internal validation estimates, not independent experimental proof
or an unbiased guarantee of future query performance.

Limitations and reproducibility
Only 96 outcomes constrain many possible sequence effects; the extensively
changed sequences have few close neighbors. Their ranking is particularly
uncertain and unstable across splits. Arbitrary epistasis, assay noise, and
sparse substitution coverage cannot be identified reliably. Fixed sequence
annotations and model configurations may miss other predictive chemistry.
There are no additional measurements to resolve these limitations. All model
random seeds are fixed and computation is restricted to one thread. The model
returns every current query ID and fits comfortably within the 120-second
per-invocation limit on this machine. All needed prediction code is contained
in proposal.py; exploration files are not imported or consumed by it.
