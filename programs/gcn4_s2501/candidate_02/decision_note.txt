Decision: deterministic support-only ordinal ensemble for GCN4_YEAST_Staller_2018.

Data and assay interpretation
The final fit uses all supplied measured records: the 32 original single mutants and the 64 independently sampled feedback variants. On each invocation, the program fits new models using only that invocation's case['support']; it never opens files, loads fitted states, or looks up stored fitness values or IDs. Query sequences provide prediction features and the population for ordinal aggregation, never outcomes. All query IDs receive finite scores in [-1,1], and selected_ids is empty. Absolute values have only ordinal meaning. The supplied assay context identifies Binding/FACS in yeast and higher ProteinGym DMS_score as better. There is no measured WT anchor, no probability interpretation, and no assumption that the units are logarithmic activity or that predictions establish wet-lab improvement. Observed zero and high fitness values are retained.

Sequence hypotheses and model
Features use the assay's variable segment, reference positions 101--144. They summarize amino acid composition, acidic/basic/aromatic/aliphatic residue counts, Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy, mutation burden, counts in overlapping sequence windows, residue clustering, and distances between acidic and bulky or aromatic residues. Windows distinguish the sequence before, around, and after the reference WxxLF segment. These are sequence-derived hypotheses about residue arrangement and context, not known three-dimensional contacts or experimentally established mechanisms. Their effects are learned with no imposed favorable sign. In particular, aromatic enrichment was not assumed to improve this assay; observed small-mutant feedback often had the opposite association.

The final seven-component ensemble comprises four ExtraTrees regressors (320 trees, leaf minima 2 and 4), two shallow Huber gradient boosting regressors (150 stages, depth 2, leaf minimum 10), and one ridge regression (alpha 1). The trees capture nonlinear combinations; the linear component supplies a smoother, complementary estimate. Four components fit standardized raw fitness and three fit support ranks. Raw targets preserve large effects, while ranks and Huber losses limit the influence of extreme magnitudes. Single-mutant sample weights are 2 for rank ExtraTrees, 0.5 for raw ExtraTrees and ridge, and 1 for boosting. These are validation-selected pooling choices, not claims about measurement quality. Regional features are used by ExtraTrees/ridge; boosting additionally uses overall composition and clustering/proximity features. Each component's query predictions are converted to fractional ranks, averaged equally, and ranked again into [-1,1]. Average ranks preserve ties. No coefficient, tree, support outcome, or query prediction is embedded in the source. All random models use seed 991 and one computation thread. Sorting records before fitting makes results invariant to record order.

Validation
Exploration compared composition-only and regional ridge regressions, positional/identity kernels, radial kernels, compact descriptors, polynomial and mutation-class interactions, ExtraTrees, random forests, and robust boosting. The main selection protocol used four independent shuffled four-fold partitions of the 64 feedback records, always retaining the initial single-mutant supports in each training set. Features/scalers and target transformations were fitted only to the training outcomes. Both overall signed Spearman correlation and small-mutant versus extensively mutated subgroup correlations were examined. Composition-only models and fine positional kernels were less useful; region-aware nonlinear models were consistently stronger. The best regional ExtraTrees configuration averaged approximately 0.424 signed Spearman in this selection protocol. Smaller leaves, target transformations, and pooling weights were tested rather than assuming additive single-mutant extrapolation.

A further three fresh four-fold partitions checked a short list and motivated an equal-rank ensemble. The seven-component precursor ensemble had pooled-feedback Spearman correlations approximately 0.524, 0.536, and 0.588. These checks also informed ensemble selection and therefore are not an untouched external test.

The exact final production entry point, including deterministic sorting and rank aggregation over unlabeled queries, was then checked on four fresh random holdouts per training size while retaining initial singles. With 48 feedback training records and 16 withheld, correlations were 0.762, 0.550, 0.453, and 0.715 (mean 0.620). With 32 feedback training records, mean correlation was 0.488; with 16, it was 0.390. This supports useful feedback learning and graceful degradation with smaller public training subsets. These are internal estimates from few noisy supports, not estimates of hidden-query accuracy. Exact query coverage, finite bounds, empty selections, repeated-call determinism, and support/query order invariance passed. Three full fits took approximately 8.1 seconds in the local environment, comfortably below the 120-second fit limit.

Limitations
Only 96 outcomes are available, and extensive rearrangements have sparse coverage. Several configurations and ensembles were compared, so selection optimism remains even after fresh splits. Errors on unusual compositions, unseen substitutions, and genuinely new interaction patterns can be substantial. Rankings within extensively altered variants are less secure than those within small mutant sets. No external sequence, MSA, structure, pretrained model, hidden outcome, or further measurement is used. Feature associations do not identify causal biophysical mechanisms. The result is a ranking proposal for this assay and candidate pool, without calibrated uncertainty or promised experimental improvement. Exploration scripts and logs have been left in place; they are not dependencies of the predictor.
