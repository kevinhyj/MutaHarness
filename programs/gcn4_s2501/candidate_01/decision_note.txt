Decision: deploy the deterministic, support-only ordinal ensemble in proposal.py.

Assay and objective
The supplied GCN4_YEAST_Staller_2018 context describes a FACS binding endpoint with ProteinGym DMS_score in original units. Higher observed fitness is the target orientation. The program does not infer probabilities, assume a measured wild-type value, or claim that predicted variants improve a wet-lab endpoint. It converts observed outcomes to empirical ranks within each actual training set; this preserves direction while limiting the leverage of unusually low and high measurements. Final scores are centered empirical ranks of predictions, strictly within [-1, 1]. They are arbitrary ordinal values.

Measured feedback
All 96 supplied observed supports are used for the delivered fit: 32 original single mutants and 64 independently sampled feedback variants. The latter provide direct evidence about multi-mutation behavior, including heavily rewritten sequences. No additional measurements or outcomes were requested. Every invocation builds features and refits exclusively from the support argument. No records, fitted coefficients, prediction IDs, or trained states are embedded or read from disk. The query sequences are only transformed with fixed descriptors and training-set normalization; they do not participate in model fitting. The assignment is not used to exclude current query records, and selected_ids is always empty.

Sequence hypotheses and model
Only residues 101--144 vary in this case. Sequence descriptors count acidic, basic, aromatic, broadly hydrophobic, proline, glycine, and polar residues. Fixed windows distinguish the supplied reference's WxxLF neighborhood at 120--124 and its flanks; maximal local counts in windows of 5, 9, and 15 residues encode clustering. Low-frequency cosine profiles encode smooth positional distributions without requiring structural coordinates. These descriptors are hypotheses about useful sequence patterns, not experimentally demonstrated folding, exposure, disorder, or binding mechanisms. Model coefficients and relationships, including their signs, are learned from measured supports.

Four seeded tree models (random forest and extra trees, each with single-mutant weights 0.3 and 1) capture nonlinear combinations and supply 25% of the prediction. The other 75% uses regularized kernel experts: lightly mutated sequences use a four-model Gaussian ensemble of smooth profiles, two bandwidths, and two global-composition weights. Sequences with more than 15 differences from reference use an average of a strongly regularized linear model and Gaussian model trained on the observed high-distance supports, provided at least eight such supports exist. This regime distinction reflects the clear library distance gap, and allows the relationships to differ rather than assuming single-mutant effects add across 40 substitutions. Local experts use conditional empirical ranks; trees provide a shared cross-regime component. When high-distance evidence is insufficient the shared Gaussian ensemble is used instead. All hyperparameters and ensemble weights are documented constants selected during development, never fitted states. Seeds, single-thread execution, and canonical support ordering make repeated calls deterministic. Typical full fit took about one second here, well below 120 seconds.

Validation
Development compared composition-only models, regional composition, categorical position effects, property profiles, residue-pair environments, clustering features, distance interactions, split-regime models, Gaussian and polynomial kernels, and tree/boosting families. Raw, robustly clipped, and ranked targets were compared. The development validation used three repeated four-fold splits of feedback variants stratified by low/high mutation distance; original singles remained available in training. Feature normalization and outcome rank transforms were fit only inside each training fold. Compact clustering and smooth profiles outperformed large categorical models, and ranked targets generally performed best. Complementary nonlinear trees and smooth local experts motivated the final ensemble. The development estimate near 0.64 was selected from many configurations and is optimistic.

The frozen complete predictor was subsequently evaluated with three fresh four-fold split seeds (109, 233, 419). Signed Spearman correlations over the 64 feedback records were 0.498, 0.447, and 0.397, averaging 0.447. Mean within-regime correlations were 0.537 for lightly mutated and 0.315 for extensively rewritten variants. These are resampling estimates on the same 96 measured supports, not an independent experimental test or a fully nested evaluation of all feature selection. Their variation and decline from development demonstrate material selection uncertainty. No query outcomes were available or used. final_validation.json records these checks. Public training-subset contract checks used 32, 48, 64, and 96 supports and confirmed complete finite bounded outputs. Repeated full-case fits produced identical dictionaries.

Limitations
The support sample is small and single-mutant effects are noisy. Only 28 feedback variants are heavily rewritten, so nonlinear interactions and transfer to novel combinations remain uncertain. Fixed sequence annotations and the distance threshold are approximations; conditional rank experts do not establish a physical cross-regime calibration. The model does not resolve true mechanisms or experimentally demonstrate higher fitness. Model selection used observed data and internal validation, so the estimates cannot be treated as a guaranteed query ranking correlation. The predictor remains entirely refittable on independent public support subsets and makes no hidden-label or external-information assumptions.
