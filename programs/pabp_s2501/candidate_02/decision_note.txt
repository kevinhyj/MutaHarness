The delivered artifact is proposal.py, with no runtime dependency on exploration
files. propose(case, assignment) refits five kernel ridge models from the supplied
support records on every invocation. It returns finite ordinal scores for every
current query ID and selected_ids=[], since the measurement budget is zero. No
fitness values, learned coefficients, variant IDs, fitted states or prediction
lists are embedded. All numerical operations are deterministic; development
validation uses explicit fixed seeds. Runtime uses no stochastic operations.

Assay interpretation
The supplied assay is PABP_YEAST_Melamed_2013, yeast PAB1 growth/essential function
and RNA binding. Higher observed DMS_score is better. These measurements are not
probabilities. No measured wild type is supplied, and no WT value is invented.
The model uses square-root and offset-log transformations as alternative monotone
latent combination scales. Offsets regularize low assay values; they do not
establish multiplicative biology. Its final output is a rank transform in [-1,1].
Only score ordering has meaning, including the signed direction of that ordering.

Measured feedback and model
The current case has 32 original singles and 64 independently sampled feedback
variants. All 96 outcomes are used in the final fit with equal observation weight.
Feedback learns effects at sites absent from the original singles, substitution
differences, and effects of combinations. Mutation features are parsed from the
case. Kernels use exact position indicators, short-range sequence-position
smoothing (length 1.5 residues), and position-specific chemical descriptors:
hydropathy, volume, polarity, charge and amino-acid group indicators. Changes,
absolute changes, and destination descriptors distinguish substitutions. A weak
global chemical component allows transfer to sites with little direct evidence.
The ordinary chemical constants in code are assumptions, not external sequence
data or pretrained scores. All fitted effects come from the supplied supports.

Three ensemble members are additive kernels and two add an RBF residual kernel
over variant distance, permitting combination-dependent effects. Different
regularization strengths and monotone outcome scales are averaged after scaling
each component by its training-target standard deviation. Kernel centering,
target scaling and coefficients all use training supports only. Query sequences
merely define feature coordinates; query outcomes are never used. Queries cannot
change the support kernel. Equal model weights reduce dependence on a narrow
hyperparameter optimum. All constants and configurations are explicit in code.

Validation and model selection
Exploration compared position/substitution kernels; local smoothing; raw, square
root, log and rank outcome scales; multiple ridge strengths; chemical descriptor
ablations; amino-acid RBF kernels; quadratic and variant-level RBF interactions;
and broad sharing between approximate RRM secondary-structure segments. The
segment and topology annotations were hypotheses based on sequence/domain
knowledge, not structure coordinates. They added little and are absent from the
delivered predictor. Exact substitution indicators alone were also insufficient.
Random forests and extremely randomized trees using ordered mutation sites and
source/destination chemistry were tested with fixed seed 1977, 150 trees, varied
leaf sizes and feature fractions. Their best mean correlation was about 0.40,
substantially below the kernel approach; they were excluded from the ensemble.

Development used seeds 17, 42 and 93 for repeated four-fold mixed-support splits
and four-fold multi-mutant holdouts with singles retained. Position models reached
about 0.59 mean signed Spearman; position-specific chemistry about 0.67; nonlinear
residual candidates about 0.68. These are model-selection statistics, not unbiased
prospective performance estimates.

A small set of ensemble options was assessed using new seeds 123, 321 and 271.
The final equal-weight ensemble gave mean Spearman 0.6478 over 12 mixed-support
folds and 0.6940 over 12 multi-mutant holdout folds with singles retained. Over six
half-support splits it gave 0.4718. A retrospective fit with only the original
singles ranked the observed feedback multi-mutants at 0.4765. Split-to-split
variation was substantial (about 0.146 standard deviation across the combined
new-seed splits). The new seeds were not used in the broad configuration search,
but the same finite support pool and a small ensemble comparison were reused;
these figures are therefore internal validation, not independent experimental
confirmation. Exploration scripts and logs are left in the workspace.

Limits and verification
Sparse observations at many sites, unobserved substitution types, assay noise,
and pair-specific epistasis remain substantial limits. Short-range smoothing is
sequence proximity, not a physical contact map. Chemical transfer and residual
kernel similarity do not identify mechanisms. Growth ranking need not imply an
isolated RNA-binding affinity ranking. No external sequences, MSA, structure,
pretrained predictions, hidden outcomes, or additional measurements are used.
Direct checks verify exact current-query coverage, finite bounded scores, empty
selection, deterministic repetition, and successful fresh fits on support subsets
including sizes 0, 1, 16, 32 and 48. Final kernels match the validation kernels
exactly. The required protected executor result is written to trial_output.json.
