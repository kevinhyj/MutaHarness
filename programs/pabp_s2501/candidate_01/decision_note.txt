Decision: rank every current unmeasured variant with a deterministic ensemble in proposal.py. No variants are selected for measurement. Higher scores mean higher predicted assay fitness; scores are within-query ordinal ranks in [-1,1], not probabilities, calibrated fitness, or evidence of wet-lab improvement.

Data and feedback
The current supplied case has 96 measured supports (32 original single mutants and 64 independently sampled feedback variants) and 186 queries. Every invocation fits from the support argument alone, giving each measured record equal weight. The feedback directly informs site effects, substitution chemistry, tolerated mutation combinations, and the tree regressors. There are no stored training records, learned coefficients, fitted states, external sequences, pretrained scores, hidden labels, or prediction lists in the predictor. Query fitness fields, if present in validation records, are ignored. All required inference code is in proposal.py. Exploration files are retained but are never read by propose.

Assay interpretation
This is the supplied yeast PAB1 growth/essential-function and RNA-binding assay. The observed fitness values are positive, higher is better, and no measured WT is assumed. Six regularized covariance regressors fit log(fitness + 0.01) or log(fitness + 0.05), with the intercept estimated from the training supports. These softened log scales allow approximate multiplicative combination effects and compress extreme outcomes without forcing WT fitness to one. Two ExtraTrees regressors fit raw fitness and training-only empirical ranks, capturing nonlinear combinations and providing a complementary scale assumption. The output combines member ranks with 65% covariance ensemble and 35% tree ensemble weight, then returns bounded ordinal scores. No nonlinear combination is interpreted as an experimentally established biochemical interaction.

Sequence features and scientific assumptions
Mutation identities are recovered by comparing supplied sequences to the supplied reference, with mutation-string parsing as fallback. Covariance components include exact position, exact substitution, signed and absolute physicochemical changes, local positional smoothing, broad hypothesized RRM segments, and nonlinear similarities between mutated amino acids. Hydropathy, residue volumes, coarse charge and amino-acid categories are ordinary scientific constants.
The reference contains repeated RRM-like segments. A deterministic sequence alignment compares segments within this same reference; it does not use an external MSA. Relative chemical compatibility with these internal repeats supplies weak covariance and tree descriptors. The segment boundaries, strand parity and alignments are sequence-derived assumptions, not structure coordinates or experimentally verified mechanisms. Strong structural constraints were not imposed. Learned predictive associations need not reflect folding or RNA-binding mechanisms.

Validation and selection
Development compared raw, softened-log and empirical-rank responses; hierarchical covariance strengths and smoothing widths; nonlinear substitution similarities and second-order covariance terms; intercept/count adjustments; and ExtraTrees, random forests and histogram boosting. Four shuffled four-fold feedback validations retained original supports in training and held out feedback variants; a separate five-fold mixed-support check assessed withholding singles too. Configuration selection favored signed Spearman correlation and consistency across splits. Six diverse covariance configurations were retained. A modest tree blend improved the ensemble despite weaker standalone tree performance. Fixed numerical configurations in proposal.py are validation-selected hyperparameters, not learned coefficients.
The development OOF rank ensemble with 35% trees averaged approximately 0.758 Spearman on feedback splits, with mixed-support OOF correlation approximately 0.622. Two additional split seeds unused in covariance configuration search yielded pooled OOF correlations approximately 0.736 and 0.713, and mixed-support OOF correlation approximately 0.618. These additional splits also informed the final blend choice and therefore are not an untouched external test.
Direct checks of the actual final propose implementation, fitting anew for each fold and forming ranks only for the supplied held-out queries, gave mean four-fold signed Spearman 0.721 (seed 1031) and 0.703 (seed 2051). Individual-fold values and exploration outputs are retained locally. Internal validation and repeated reuse of the same small dataset incur selection optimism; they do not establish performance on the unmeasured queries or wet-lab improvement.

Reusability and runtime checks
The fit recomputes centering, response transforms, coefficients, training ranks and trees from supplied supports on every call. Support records are sorted for order stability; tree random_state is 73 and numerical thread counts are limited to one. Repeated full invocations produced identical outputs. A separate 24-support invocation successfully scored a different 16-record query set. Two full invocations together took approximately 2.7 seconds locally, far below the 120-second fit budget. Empty queries and fewer than two supports have explicit deterministic fallbacks. The required executor check verifies exactly all current query IDs, finite bounded scores, and selected_ids=[].

Limitations
Only 96 labels are available, exact substitutions and double-mutant combinations are sparsely covered, and many query substitutions have little local measured evidence. Learned pooling, assumed repeat alignments and region boundaries can be wrong. Withholding single mutants was harder than withholding feedback, indicating sensitivity to training coverage. Individual ranks, especially in sparsely sampled sites or close predicted ties, remain uncertain. There is no defensible calibrated WT baseline, experimental improvement claim, or validated mechanism. No further measurements are requested.
