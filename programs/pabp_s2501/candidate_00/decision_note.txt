Final decision: a deterministic, three-member regularized additive mutation-kernel ensemble. Every call to propose(case, assignment) rebuilds all features, transforms the current support fitness values, solves three fresh regularized regressions, and scores every query. It returns selected_ids=[] because there is no remaining measurement budget. The program contains no stored support outcomes, fitted coefficients, prediction IDs, or fitted caches. It needs only NumPy and SciPy; no randomness is used.

Assay interpretation
The endpoint is yeast growth associated with essential PAB1 function and RNA binding. The ranking direction is higher observed DMS_score. No measured wild-type value is supplied or assumed. A shifted log link, log(max(fitness,0)+offset), was selected empirically on these positive supports; offsets 0.03 and 0.1 are assay-unit modeling parameters. They stabilize the low end and permit approximately multiplicative combination behavior. This link does not establish that the assay is a calibrated ratio, that the underlying biology is multiplicative, or that predicted variants improve growth experimentally. Final values are ensemble ranks mapped monotonically into (-1,1), not probabilities or calibrated fitness estimates.

Use of measured feedback
The supplied case has 32 original single-mutant supports plus 64 independently sampled feedback variants, including two additional single mutants. All 96 are used in the final fit. The feedback doubles constrain combinations directly, separate some site and substitution effects, and reveal whether a scale transformation predicts combinations better. On public training subsets, the model uses exactly the supplied supports and no outcomes from omitted records. Queries contribute sequence features only; they never affect target scaling, coefficients, or the learned intercept.

Model and assumptions
Each variant is represented by its substitutions. A variant kernel sums pairwise kernels between its constituent substitutions. Components are exact site identity, exact site/destination amino-acid identity, site-specific linear chemical changes, nonlinear similarity of chemical changes at the same site, and weak transfer between neighboring sequence positions with a Gaussian bandwidth of two residues. Chemical descriptors include standard Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy, side-chain volume, charge, polarity, and aromatic/proline/glycine/polar flags. All scales are fixed scientific descriptor normalizations, not fitted external predictors. Chemical flags allow nonlinear substitution sharing; they do not assert a folding or binding mechanism. Sequence proximity is only a predictive prior and is not a claim about spatial contacts.

The first model uses linear chemistry and local nonlinear sharing. The second uses same-site and local nonlinear sharing. The third uses a more strongly regularized same-site nonlinear kernel and a generalized-least-squares free intercept. The other two use the mean transformed support outcome as the shrinkage baseline. Each member's predictions are standardized by its current support target mean and standard deviation, then combined with fixed weights 0.4, 0.3, 0.3. The ensemble retains model diversity and avoids relying on a sharply optimized single configuration. The intercept in the third model is learned from supports and is not a measured wild-type outcome.

Scientific development and validation
Exploration files are retained. Validation used signed Spearman rank correlation with positive assay direction, recomputing label transforms and regression parameters within each training fold. Initial development used three repetitions of four-fold held-out double-mutant validation with measured singles retained, plus two repetitions of four-fold random support splits. This was used to compare raw, square-root, shifted-log and rank targets, regularization, chemical/exact substitution components, local transfer, nonlinear kernels, polynomial interaction kernels, trees, and pairwise ranking. Shifted-log additive kernels were strongest. Trees and the direct pairwise ranker were weaker. Polynomial epistasis, pooled chemical effects, and tentative RRM sequence motif/secondary-structure groupings did not provide sufficient stable evidence to include them. The final predictor contains no structural annotation.

After defining the four candidate kernel models and coarse ensemble alternatives, fresh seeds 401, 809 and 1327 were used for three repetitions of: (i) four-fold held-out feedback validation preserving exactly the original 32 supports, (ii) four-fold random splits of all supports, and (iii) four random 48-support training subsets. The selected robust three-model blend gave mean signed Spearman correlations of approximately 0.728, 0.655, and 0.588, respectively. Those are internal estimates on measured records, with substantial variability (overall fold standard deviation about 0.129). They are not hidden-query scores, experimental validation, or an unbiased final test: model and ensemble choices were informed by these supports, and the many exploratory comparisons create selection optimism. Fresh split seeds reduce dependence on one partition but do not create new independent experimental data.

Verification and limitations
The implementation was checked for repeat determinism, finite bounded scores, complete current query coverage, and refitting with 1, 16, 32, 48 and 72 support records. Its compact mutation-kernel computation was compared numerically with the independently explored feature calculations. The required protected executor is run separately as the final artifact and contract check; trial_output.json records its result.

Only 96 outcomes span many different positions and substitutions. Unobserved sites and substitutions therefore rely heavily on weak sequence-local and chemical transfer assumptions. Specific epistasis, distant contacts, stability thresholds and RNA-binding mechanisms remain poorly identifiable. Shifted-log regression expresses a useful combination hypothesis without learning an experimentally established mechanism. No external sequences, pretrained scores, MSA, structure coordinates, hidden labels, previous evaluation results, or additional measurements were used. Rank outputs do not include calibrated uncertainty; close scores should not be interpreted as confident differences. No wet-lab improvement claim is made.
