Final decision: deterministic equal-rank ensemble of two ExtraTrees regressors and one regularized nonlinear global-response model. proposal.py contains all runtime code. propose(case, assignment) refits every component solely from the support records in that invocation, scores every current query ID, and returns selected_ids=[]. No IDs, assay outcomes, fitted coefficients, prediction lists, or cached models are embedded in the predictor. No runtime file reads or external data are used. Trees use seed 1729, 500 estimators each, single-thread execution, max_features=0.8, and leaf size 1. Query scores are finite centered midranks in [-1,1]; their absolute values carry no assay calibration.

Measured feedback: The supplied case has 32 original single mutants and 64 independently sampled feedback variants. All 96 outcomes inform the final fit with equal sample weight; their source IDs do not enter features. The feedback reveals large heterogeneity among combinations and mutation loads. A bright eight-mutation support demonstrates why a simple mutation-count ranking is inadequate. Features pool mutation chemistry by sums and maxima, retain mutation load and regional burdens, and allow nonlinear interactions in the trees. Original singles supply direct local and generic substitution-tolerance evidence, while feedback teaches how these properties relate to combinations. No further measurements are requested.

Assay interpretation: Higher observed fluorescence/DMS_score is always better. Observed low values cluster near 1.30, whereas many bright supports lie near 3.2-3.9. These observations motivate a saturating response, but the units are not assumed to be calibrated fluorescence probabilities, thermodynamic energies, or WT-relative improvements. For the nonlinear component, the lower response endpoint is each training set's fourth percentile and the upper endpoint is its 95th percentile plus a fixed 0.12 margin. These are nuisance modeling choices, not measurements of WT or physical assay bounds. Logistic response fitting minimizes squared response residuals with an L2 penalty of 0.3; its latent response is ranked. SVD reduces optimization to supported feature directions. No measured WT is assumed.

Features and hypotheses: Constants include Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy, standard amino-acid volumes, approximate charge categories, and chemical substitution classes. Sequence differences are derived against the supplied reference. The first tree uses local and alternating-position sequence hydropathy, broad sequence regions, and conservative/radical substitution burdens. The second tree and nonlinear component add residue identity and tentative beta-like segment/alternating-face annotations, central-helix and chromophore-region flags, and chemistry-by-environment products. These assumed annotations require no coordinates and are deliberately approximate; they are predictive hypotheses, not a verified structure or mechanism. Their inclusion was evaluated against a representation that does not require those annotations. Pooled maxima and tree interactions can capture dominant damaging substitutions and nonlinear combinations, but cannot identify all specific pairwise epistasis from these data.

Validation: Every held-out prediction was fitted without that record's outcome. Development used three seeded repetitions of four-fold feedback holdout (original singles retained in training), plus a four-fold mixed-support holdout. Candidates included tree depth/leaf variants, ridge regressions, sparse mutation/site/substitution effects, sigmoid global-response models, and a constrained per-mutation damage model. Sparse effects and heavily parameterized damage fits generalized less well. The selected ensemble combined generic chemistry trees, annotation-enriched trees, and a sigmoid global-response model with equal rank weights, improving both mean feedback correlation and stability relative to individual members. On development splits its mean signed Spearman was approximately 0.637 for feedback holdouts and 0.695 for mixed-support holdouts. The selected ensemble was then checked on two fresh seeded four-fold feedback splits and one fresh mixed-support split: mean correlations were approximately 0.655 and 0.606, respectively. These checks used 400 trees versus the final 500-tree variance-reduction setting. Development model searches reused splits and are selection-biased; fresh splits still reuse the same small collection of measured proteins, so neither result constitutes an independent experimental test. The exploratory scripts and logs are left in the workspace.

Limitations: Only 96 supports are available, most specific substitutions are sparsely observed, and high-order variants require extrapolation. Floor-level measurements have extremely small differences that may reflect measurement noise and are particularly difficult to rank. No uncertainty calibration or probability interpretation is claimed. No external sequences, pretrained predictor scores, MSA, coordinates, or hidden query outcomes were used. Internal correlations support a modeling choice for the signed rank objective; they do not establish folding mechanisms, interaction mechanisms, or wet-lab improvement. Public subset executions always learn from their own supplied supports; with fewer or compositionally different supports, performance can change substantially.

Artifact verification: Direct repeated invocation produced identical scores for all 186 current queries in approximately 1.5 seconds per fit locally. The required protected executor check is run after this note is saved, with its output retained in trial_output.json.
