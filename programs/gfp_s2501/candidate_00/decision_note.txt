Final decision: support-only deterministic ordinal ensemble in proposal.py.

Data and assay interpretation
The supplied case has 32 original single mutants and 64 independently sampled measured feedback variants; all 96 outcomes train the final invocation. The feedback includes one additional single mutant, so validation by mutation order separates 33 single mutants and 63 multi-mutants. Higher ProteinGym assay fitness means higher fluorescence ranking. The observed scale has a dense floor near 1.301 and a bright cluster around 3–4. This motivates a saturating response, but neither a measured WT nor a calibrated fluorescence transformation is assumed. Scores are query-relative ranks mapped strictly inside [-1,1], not probabilities or claims of experimental improvement. No further measurements are selected.

Model
Sequence differences against the supplied reference define mutations. Shared features include mutation count, squared burden, physicochemical changes in hydropathy, volume and charge, proline/glycine changes, aromatic losses, conservative substitutions, maxima, and selected squared damage summaries. Additional regularized features represent positions, position-specific chemical changes, substitution types, and source/target residue counts. These let measured feedback alter substitution/general damage effects rather than merely penalizing mutation number.

The ensemble combines an additive kernel regression, two smooth nonlinear kernels, and a regularized global-epistasis model with a sigmoid assay response (assumed floor 1.30 and upper scale 4.0). The latter learns a latent score jointly from all supplied supports; it does not add independently fitted single-mutant fluorescence values. Nonlinear shared features and smooth kernels permit combination effects without fitting a largely unidentifiable explicit pairwise mutation table. Each component is fitted anew and converted to ordinal ranks before combination.

Approximate GFP strand intervals, alternating strand orientations, chromophore-region annotations, and a local alternating hydropathy proxy are scientific hypotheses, not coordinate-derived structure. The final ensemble puts 75% weight on a configuration with most strand/burial features removed, and 25% on the fuller hypothesis set. The reduced model still retains a proline-in-beta squared summary and chemical-core descriptors; its fixed ablation also removes one hydrophobic-loss squared term. This is an empirically tested feature ablation, not a definitive test of GFP structural biology. Kernel substitution-block weight is 0.3 and regularization is 0.3. The strong nonlinear kernel receives less weight below 65 supports because low-feedback validation showed greater instability. These configurations are fixed; no support outcomes or learned coefficients are embedded in code.

Validation
Exploration compared ridge regression, raw/rank/transformed targets, extra trees, additive kernels, nonlinear kernels, global epistasis and pairwise ranking. Initial repeated four-fold multi-feedback validation used seeds 12,47,103, fitting all available singles plus the remaining multi-mutants on each fold. Best individual candidates had mean signed Spearman around 0.68–0.70; tree and pairwise approaches were generally weaker. An equal-family full-feature ensemble reached approximately 0.701 in these development splits.

Additional seeds 209,317,421 evaluated multi-feedback holdouts, all-support holdouts, and training with only 16 multi-feedback examples. Full structural features gave about 0.658 for the equal-family ensemble on multi-feedback holdouts, versus 0.676 with the reduced feature configuration; all-support validation slightly favored the full features (about 0.687 versus 0.682). Therefore structural assumptions received limited rather than decisive weight. Simple negative mutation count had approximately 0.604 on the additional multi-feedback splits. The actual final ensemble was then fitted independently on the 12 additional multi-feedback splits: mean signed Spearman 0.666, fold standard deviation 0.114. This is internal predictive validation on observed supports, not an unbiased independent performance guarantee: model-family and configuration choices used this small dataset, folds overlap, and final settings were informed by validation. Validation and exploration files remain in the workspace.

Execution and limitations
proposal.py is self-contained and receives all data through propose(case, assignment). It makes no file reads, network calls, measurement requests, pretrained-score calls, or fitted-state cache accesses. Support filtering, feature scales, kernel fits and nonlinear response parameters are recomputed every invocation. The algorithms are deterministic; development tree/split comparisons used explicit seeds, while the final estimator uses no stochastic fitting. Direct checks verified identical repeated outputs, every query ID, finite scores within the required bounds, empty selected_ids, and fresh fits on smaller support subsets. Local full fits took approximately 0.04 seconds, well below the 120-second limit.

The sample is small, substitutions are sparse and many query sites/effects remain unseen. Low-fluorescence values are floor-compressed and may include measurement noise, limiting their ordering. Dense nonlinear effects and specific physical interactions are not identifiable reliably from 96 outcomes. Sequence-derived burial, thresholds, and latent effects remain modeling assumptions, with uncertainty especially for high mutation counts, unseen substitutions, and spectral/chromophore changes. Ranking is for this assay only.
