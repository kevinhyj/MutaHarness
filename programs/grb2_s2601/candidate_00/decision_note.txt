Decision: support-only deterministic chemical-kernel and interaction ensemble

Target and assay interpretation
The target is signed rank correlation with the supplied higher-is-better
ProteinGym DMS_score for GRB2_HUMAN_Faure_2021 (yeast growth). The output is an
ordinal ranking of all 186 current unmeasured variants. Neither the internal
regression predictions nor the returned scores are calibrated probabilities,
and this work does not demonstrate improvement in a wet-lab assay. No measured
wild type is available or assumed: each fit estimates an unpenalized intercept.
No additional measurements are requested; selected_ids is always empty.

How feedback is used
Every invocation reconstructs substitutions relative to the case reference and
fits all coefficients using only fitness values of that invocation's supplied
support records. The full case has 32 original single-mutant records and 64
independently sampled feedback records. Original and feedback records enter the
final regression with equal observation weight; identifiers are not predictors.
The feedback identifies joint mutation burdens and substitution effects absent
from the original single-mutant coverage. Every training subset is refitted,
with no stored support values, prediction IDs, fitted models, or cached states.
The proposal does not read files or access network resources.

Model
The main component (80% ensemble weight) uses additive mutation covariances:
strong sharing within a mutated position and weaker sharing across chemically
similar wild-type residues. Substituted amino acids are compared by fixed
hydropathy, side-chain volume, charge, aromaticity, glycine/proline indicators,
polar-amide/hydroxyl class, and beta-branching descriptors. Gaussian similarities
use two nearby bandwidths (2 and 4); ridge regularization is 0.4. Weak global
composition and mutation-order features allow shared trends. These chemical
similarities are priors for generalization, not observed mechanisms.

The remaining 20% is a regularized second-order tensor-kernel ensemble. Its
base features describe changed positions, per-position property changes,
mutation severity, amino-acid and wild-type-to-mutant identities, broad residue
classes, and mutation count. Tensor covariance weights 0.2 and 0.6 permit
learned pair and other nonlinear combination effects while shrinking sparse
combinations. This does not identify specific physical contacts.

Each component is fitted on both the raw assay scale and log(fitness - floor).
The nominal floor is -2, or lower than the observed minimum if necessary. This
is a sensitivity model for flattening effects on the assay scale, not an
experimentally established lower limit. Models are centered/scaled by their
training targets before ensemble averaging. Final scores are midranks mapped
strictly inside [-1,1], preserving ties. No probability or absolute-fitness
interpretation is attached to their magnitudes.

Validation and configuration choice
Exploration compared hierarchical position/substitution ridge models, chemical
Gaussian kernels, log/logit and rank target transforms, and tensor interactions
using repeated held-out feedback and mixed-support splits. A weak hypothesized
SH3 topology/parity prior was also tested. Its advantage did not persist clearly
across fresh split seeds, so the delivered model uses no structural annotation.
Rank target transforms were excluded from the final ensemble; all final target
transformations and fitted quantities depend only on the training fold.

The saved final_validation.json records checks of the actual final predictor on
additional fixed split seeds. Original singles were retained while feedback
records were held out. With four folds, pooled signed Spearman correlations for
three seeds were 0.5870, 0.5511, 0.5956 (mean 0.5779), versus 0.4444, 0.4986,
0.5294 for a position-only ridge baseline. With two folds, they were 0.5658,
0.5720, 0.5076 (mean 0.5485), versus 0.4835, 0.4810, 0.5312. Two mixed-support
four-fold checks gave 0.6048 and 0.5320 versus baseline 0.5868 and 0.4451.
These are internal resampling diagnostics on the same 96 measured outcomes,
not independent experimental validation or an unbiased estimate of future
performance. Split variability and configuration search imply uncertainty.
Exploration scripts, reports, and the initial executable are left in place.
The final artifact is self-contained in proposal.py and uses NumPy/SciPy only.

Reproducibility and limitations
Inference has no randomized operations; exploratory split/search seeds are
explicitly recorded in the scripts. Repeated invocation, support/query order
invariance, refitting on a smaller support subset, missing/one-observation
support behavior, exact query-ID coverage, score bounds, and finite output were
checked. Typical full-case computation takes approximately 0.02 seconds locally
once imports finish, comfortably below the 120-second fit limit.

Only 96 outcomes cover a large substitution/combination space. Some query sites
have no measured substitutions, most exact substitutions and pair combinations
are unobserved, and phenotype heterogeneity may defeat chemical similarity.
The sequence alone cannot establish burial, binding contacts, folding effects,
or causes of epistasis. Strongly regularized shared features improve stability
but can miss sharp substitution-specific effects. No external sequences, MSA,
structure coordinates, pretrained scores, or hidden labels were used. The
ranking remains a support-informed statistical prediction with these limits.
