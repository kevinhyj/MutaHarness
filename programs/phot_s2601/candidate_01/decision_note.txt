Final deliverable: proposal.py, propose(case, assignment).

Target and assay interpretation
The assay is PHOT_CHLRE_Chen_2023, Phototropin fluorescence measured by FACS in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. Higher observed ProteinGym fitness is better. The objective is signed Spearman rank correlation. No WT measurement, calibrated probability, absolute improvement, or mechanistic conclusion is assumed. Final predictions are converted monotonically to centered fractional ranks in [-1,1]; selected_ids is always empty.

Use of measured feedback
Every invocation reconstructs mutation features from the supplied reference and sequences, then refits all numerical parameters using only supplied finite support outcomes. The current case has 64 independently sampled multi-mutant feedback records and 32 original single-mutant records. Their mutation distributions differ sharply: most original singles have substitutions absent from the combinatorial query design. Multi-mutant outcomes therefore train the combinatorial response surface directly; unrelated singleton measurements are not allowed to distort its intercept. Original singles train a separate, deliberately conservative, position/chemical-similarity predictor for isolated single-mutant queries. This includes A28H as a singleton anchor. The four isolated current queries have little directly matching evidence. For them, standard amino-acid hydrophobicity, volume, charge, aromaticity, proline and glycine indicators and sequence-position proximity provide a weak similarity prior, with substantial shrinkage toward the observed singleton median. These constants are biochemical descriptors, not pretrained predictions or structural coordinates. A small-support fallback uses additive ridge regression.

Scientific modeling
The final combinatorial predictor averages three independently refitted ridge models on raw assay fitness: (1) categorical substitution main effects plus all between-position, training-centered pair products, interaction feature multiplier 0.1 and ridge penalty 0.01; (2) interactions touching the site with the largest norm of support-fitted additive allele effects, multiplier 0.3 and penalty 0.03; (3) all pair products weighted continuously by the stronger of the two sites' fitted main-effect norms, multiplier 0.1 and penalty 0.03. Importance estimates come from a separate weakly regularized additive fit on the same supplied training records. The main-effect ranking and selected hub site are not stored. These represent alternative statistical shrinkage assumptions about epistasis, not demonstrated residue contacts. Centering, importance, coefficients and intercepts are recomputed in every fit. Inapplicable within-site products are excluded. Unseen substitution columns have zero training information and cannot acquire a fitted effect.

Validation and selection
Exploration files are retained. Initial comparisons used deterministic repeated four-fold validation (four repetitions, seed 2026) on the 64 combinatorial supports, scoring held-out signed Spearman correlation. Models included additive and second-order ridge, several regularization strengths, raw/log/square-root/rank/logit response scales, sequence-identity Gaussian kernels, Extra Trees, boosting, sparse elastic-net interactions, hierarchical interactions, and direct pairwise ordinal loss. Raw-response modestly regularized interactions performed best. A single explicitly restricted Q46/R59 interaction hypothesis and direct ordinal-loss models did not improve sufficiently to warrant selection. Broad models do not require an assumed physical contact map.

A subsequent outer comparison used two independently seeded four-fold partitions (809 and 1021), refitting every prediction from each outer training fold. A more adaptive ensemble reselected candidate weights through inner validation, using only those outer training outcomes. The fixed equal-weight three-model ensemble achieved mean fold Spearman 0.846691 and correlation of averaged repeated out-of-fold predictions 0.890156. The adaptive alternative achieved mean fold Spearman 0.838603; a 75/25 fixed/adaptive blend achieved 0.844118. The simpler fixed ensemble was retained. Fixed-ensemble fold correlations ranged from 0.514706 to 0.923529. The pooled averaged prediction metric uses overlapping repeated training fits and should not be interpreted as independent replication. Initial candidate screening used these same public supports, so even the subsequent comparison retains model-selection optimism; it is not an unbiased estimate of unknown-query performance. Validation supports a modeling choice, not experimental proof. No hidden outcomes or additional measurements were used.

Reproducibility, contract and limitations
All coefficients are learned afresh, with no data/model/prediction file reads, network calls, or cached states. No IDs or fitness values are embedded in the predictor. Numerical thread count is restricted to one; all validation partitions have fixed seeds; final regression has no randomness. The current full fit takes well below one second locally, leaving ample room under the 120-second independent-fit limit. The output covers every current query ID with a finite score in [-1,1] and no measurement selections.

There are only 64 relevant combination measurements, approximately 20 design substitutions, and many possible interactions. Shrinkage is essential, higher-order effects remain unmodeled, and rankings between close variants can be unstable. Single-mutant extrapolation is particularly weak, and chemical/sequence similarity is only an assumption. There are no external homologs, MSA, structure, predictor scores, hidden labels, measured WT, or biological replicate noise estimates. Statistical interaction terms and phenotype associations cannot establish fluorescence mechanisms, residue contacts, folding improvement or wet-lab benefit.
