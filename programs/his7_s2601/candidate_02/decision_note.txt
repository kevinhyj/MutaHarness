Decision: use a deterministic ordinal ensemble trained afresh on the supplied measured supports on every invocation. proposal.py contains all inference code and performs no data, model, or prediction-list file reads. All current query IDs receive finite scores in [-1,1]; selected_ids is always empty. No further measurements are requested.

Assay interpretation
The endpoint is yeast growth, and higher supplied ProteinGym DMS_score is better. We make no assumption of measured WT fitness or of a calibrated probability scale. The 32 measured singles range from approximately 0.820 to 1.185; the 64 independently sampled measured feedback combinations range from 0 to approximately 1.186, with 12 exact zeros. This discrepancy makes extrapolating single effects alone inadequate. Zero outcomes remain tied observations; they are not treated as proof of a particular molecular failure mechanism. The target is signed Spearman rank correlation, and returned values are arbitrary ordinal scores.

Use of measured feedback and final model
Every fit combines all supplied singles and feedback outcomes, with no distinction by hardcoded record ID. Mutation identities are derived from the supplied reference and sequences (mutation strings are a fallback). Unlabeled query sequences may define feature columns, but no query outcomes are accessed. The main component is ElasticNet regression of tie-aware normal scores of observed fitness ranks: alpha=0.003, l1_ratio=0.9, cyclic coordinate descent, fixed seed 2601. Features include position-specific mutant alleles, mutated-position indicators, counts in eight sequence/design windows, and window indicators for at least two mutations. Those indicators allow combination backgrounds to depart from single-mutant backgrounds. The windows reflect library co-occurrence patterns, including two overlapping sequence neighborhoods separated into their designed position sets; they are assumed annotations, not structure coordinates or validated structural domains.

The second component uses a Gaussian kernel exp(-0.8*d), where d is exact categorical Hamming distance between supplied genotypes. Different alternative alleles at one site contribute one difference, and an unseen allele is still counted correctly. This smooth nonlinear kernel models combination-dependent interpolation without explicitly fitting thousands of separate interaction coefficients. Kernel ridge regularization is 1. Its target equally combines empirical normal rank scores and standardized observed fitness. A learned intercept is used, without forcing a WT value. The final ensemble combines component query ranks with fixed weights 0.60 sparse model and 0.40 nonlinear model, then maps ensemble ranks into [-1,1]. These fixed hyperparameters and mixture weights are validation-selected configurations, not stored learned coefficients. All fitted coefficients and target transformations are recomputed from the current supports.

Scientific development and validation
A executable baseline was saved before exploration. Comparisons used only observed supports, with held-out feedback outcomes removed from training and 32 original singles retained. Repeated four-fold feedback validation examined additive and polynomial interaction kernels, feedback weighting, normal/uniform rank targets, raw-fitness and logit targets, Gaussian similarity kernels, sparse regression, gated mutation features, random forests, ExtraTrees, gradient boosting, conventional residue-property feature transfer, and regularized pairwise logistic ranking. Sparse normal-rank models generalized better than the raw-fitness tree models. Residue hydrophobicity, volume, charge, and other property features did not give a reliable improvement and were omitted. Pairwise logistic ranking and explicit interaction expansions were also weaker than the final mixture. No external sequences, pretrained scores, MSA, structure, hidden outcomes, or prior evaluation results were used.

Across nine exploratory four-fold repetitions, the selected sparse component had mean pooled feedback Spearman approximately 0.592; the Gaussian component approximately 0.570; their 60/40 rank mixture approximately 0.622. These figures informed model selection and are therefore subject to selection optimism. A separate set of random seeds checked the exact finalized propose implementation, including its output rank transformation and feature construction. With 32 singles plus 48 feedback variants for training and 16 held-out feedback variants, 12 repetitions gave mean signed Spearman 0.636 and split standard deviation 0.198 (range 0.149 to 0.890). With 32 singles plus 32 feedback variants and 32 held-out feedback variants, eight repetitions gave mean 0.546 and split standard deviation 0.081. Splits reuse the same measured records; these are sensitivity checks, not independent experiments or confidence intervals. Detailed results are in final_validation_results.json. Exploration code and results are retained in the workspace.

Remaining limitations
The sample is small, and some windows have only two to five measured combinations. Strong variation across holdouts shows uncertainty about unseen combinations. Near the functional fitness band, fine ordering can be dominated by noise, and no replicate-based assay error estimate is available. Entirely new alleles have no directly learned allele coefficient; their effects are supported only by mutated-position, window/background, and similarity features. Mutation dependencies inferred by these models are predictive hypotheses, not experimentally established mechanisms. The ensemble offers a ranking of the existing unmeasured variants, not calibrated growth estimates, causal structural claims, or evidence of wet-lab improvement. Validation cannot guarantee performance on unmeasured variants.
