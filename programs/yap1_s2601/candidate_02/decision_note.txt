The deliverable is standalone proposal.py, with propose(case, assignment). It fits anew using only the fitness values in case['support'], scores every current query ID, and always selects no variants. Nothing in the inference path reads case files, exploration logs, stored training records, cached models, or prediction lists. All 96 supplied outcomes, including the 64 feedback variants, enter the final fit. The supplied reference and variant sequences define substitutions; names/IDs do not determine predictions. No measurements are requested.

The target is signed rank correlation for the positive peptide-binding DMS readout, with larger values better. No observed wild-type value is assumed. Three ensemble members optimize pairwise logistic ordering, emphasizing pairs separated in empirical fitness rank. Two members regress square-root fitness. Square root, log, fourth root, raw fitness, inverse-hyperbolic sine, and normal-score ranks were compared during development. Square root reduced the leverage of large readings and gave stronger support validation than a strictly multiplicative log-fitness assumption. It is a modeling transform, not a demonstrated assay mechanism. Model predictions are combined as within-query percentile ranks, then mapped to ordinal scores in [-1,1]. These scores are not probabilities, calibrated fitness, or experimental improvement claims.

Each member uses a regularized kernel hierarchy: exact substitution effects; position effects; position-specific changes in chemical descriptors; shared substituted-residue identity; and pooled mutation-severity descriptors. Chemical descriptors use fixed hydropathy, volume, charge, aromaticity, proline, and glycine constants. One member also uses similarity of fixed BLOSUM62 rows as conventional amino-acid exchangeability descriptors, not a pretrained sequence predictor or an external sequence search. Shared descriptors permit cautious predictions for substitutions absent from training; effects and their signs are estimated exclusively from supplied support outcomes. Proline introduction, loss of aromatic residues, and putative beta-strand/loop context are hypotheses used as regularized features. WW-region annotations (176-181, 186-190, 196-201 as putative strands) follow the local motif and are not inferred structure coordinates or experimentally verified mechanisms. Mutation count is fitted as an additional covariate in some members. Two members use a degree-two kernel expansion to allow context-dependent combination effects, strongly sharing/shrinking them instead of estimating unrestricted individual double-mutant interactions.

The fixed five-member mixture is: 30% additive pairwise ranking; 25% pairwise ranking with mutation count; 15% degree-two pairwise ranking with mutation count; 15% additive square-root regression using amino-acid exchangeability similarity; and 15% degree-two square-root regression. All kernel weights, regularization strengths, and mixture weights are documented in proposal.py. They are support-validation-selected configurations, not stored learned mutation coefficients. Every invocation estimates its own coefficients from its current support subset. Fitting is deterministic; seed 2601 is explicit, with no stochastic inference operations.

Validation used only observed supports. The principal development design kept the 32 original single mutants in training and held out one quarter of the independently sampled feedback batch, using four folds repeated with seeds 17, 41, and 73. Additional schedules used seeds 103, 139, 197, and 251 for feedback-only holdouts, and 113, 181, and 277 for all-support holdouts. Each validation call refitted the actual artifact on the corresponding subset. The final ensemble's mean signed Spearman correlation was 0.639 on the development schedule, 0.620 on the additional feedback schedule (fold standard deviation 0.157), and 0.627 on the all-support schedule (fold standard deviation 0.114). Training on the original 32 singles alone and predicting the entire measured feedback batch gave 0.592; this different split is a descriptive comparison, not a paired or causal estimate of feedback benefit. Feedback-heldout fits use 80 supports, all-support-heldout fits use 72, and the delivered query fit uses all 96. Comparisons included multiple transforms, local chemical/similarity models, broader cross-position sharing, additive versus polynomial kernels, and pairwise rank objectives. Broad substituted-residue sharing and rank fitting were retained for robust performance across designs; proposed regional transfer features did not justify stronger weighting.

These are internal model-selection and robustness estimates, not an independent experimental test. Additional schedules were inspected while deciding the final ensemble, so their scores are not a completely untouched benchmark. Repeated folds overlap; fold standard deviations are descriptive and are not confidence intervals. The query distribution is mostly double mutants, and many substitutions have no direct measured example. The sparse design confounds some single-mutation effects with their partners. It cannot establish physical epistasis, folding energetics, a structural mechanism, or wet-lab improvement. Prediction uncertainty is largest for new substitutions and sparsely sampled sites. Global chemical sharing and nonlinear kernels are inductive assumptions constrained by regularization and validation, not observed biological facts.

Local verification of the finished artifact confirmed identical outputs on repeated fits, finite scores within bounds, coverage of all 186 current query IDs, and selected_ids=[]; full fitting took approximately 0.05 seconds with single-thread BLAS in the local environment. Development scripts and exploration artifacts have been retained. The final required protected-executor contract check is run after this note is written; its output is trial_output.json.
