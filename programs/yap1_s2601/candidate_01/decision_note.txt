This deliverable ranks the existing unmeasured YAP1 variants for the peptide-binding assay YAP1_HUMAN_Araya_2012. Higher observed fitness defines the positive ranking direction. The returned scores are ordinal values in [-1,1], not probabilities, calibrated assay values, or a claim that any variant improves wet-lab binding. selected_ids is always empty; no measurements are requested.

Data use and refitting
The provided case has 96 measured supports: 32 original single mutants and 64 independently sampled feedback variants. All 96 outcomes enter every final fit, with equal weight. Every invocation obtains its labels exclusively from case['support']; reference and variant sequences come exclusively from the supplied case. It recomputes all coefficients, intercepts and kernel centering from that invocation's support subset. There are no stored training labels, learned coefficients, variant IDs, pretrained scores, external sequences, MSA or coordinates, and no file reads in proposal.py. Query records supply feature vectors and final ordinal normalization only; kernel normalization and learned quantities use support rows. The model performs no stochastic operations. Exploratory validation uses explicit fixed seeds. Runtime is far below the 120-second fitting budget with local numpy/scipy.

Assay interpretation
Observed fitness is positive and ranges approximately from 0.033 to 4.891. An additive model in log fitness is a plausible multiplicative combination null for enrichment-like binding readouts. It is a predictive assumption, not an established assay mechanism. We compared log, log(fitness+0.1), square-root, raw and normal-rank targets. Log-based members performed better in the exploratory grids. The 0.1-offset members reduce the leverage of outcomes near the observed floor; this offset is a validation-selected modeling constant, not a measured background or detection limit. A learned intercept avoids assuming a measured WT outcome. For reusability, nonpositive outcomes trigger a support-scaled signed asinh transform.

Final model
Five equally weighted kernel ridge members predict transformed fitness. Features combine mutation position, exact substitution identity, position-specific chemical changes, and global chemical changes. Chemical descriptors are ordinary hydropathy, approximate residue volume, charge and sidechain indicators; they are not pretrained predictor scores. Hierarchical features share changes between identical reference amino acids and between overlapping reference chemical categories. This lets measured feedback contribute both exact substitution evidence and transfer to unobserved substitutions. Coverage matters: 45 current queries have all component substitutions observed, 90 have one unobserved component, and 51 have two unobserved components.

Four members include a positive-semidefinite squared additive kernel, allowing regularized smooth interactions between changes; one is additive and shares exact reference-to-destination substitution effects. Members vary chemical pooling, regularization and log offset. Their output ranks are averaged and ranked again to produce bounded ordinal scores. This limits dependence on output-scale choices and any single nonlinear specification. Hyperparameters are documented constants selected from support-only validation, not hardcoded fitted states. No arbitrary pair-specific mechanistic corrections are imposed. Approximate WW strand/loop annotations were considered in exploration but excluded from the final model; chemistry-based sharing provided stronger and simpler validation support.

Validation and limitations
Development compared local additive, chemical similarity, hierarchical pooling and second-order kernel variants using repeated four-fold splits. One validation regime retained all original 32 singles while holding out feedback records; the other split all supports. Exploratory selection emphasized the feedback regime because the query pool is overwhelmingly double mutants. All held-out labels were excluded from coefficient fitting and target transformations. The final checks invoke the actual propose function with training subsets and remove fitness from held-out query records, so they exercise the reusable artifact rather than only an approximation to its training code.

Final repeated-split diagnostics: feedback holdout mean signed Spearman 0.5944 across 20 folds (fold SD 0.1718); mixed-support holdout mean 0.5414 across 12 folds (fold SD 0.1602). Six mutation-token holdout stress splits ensure every test variant has at least one substitution absent from training: mean 0.6138, split SD 0.1370, with 67-75 training records and 21-29 test records. These SDs describe variation among overlapping splits, not confidence intervals. The same small support collection informed model development and later diagnostics; even new random splits are not an independent experimental test, and configuration selection can inflate reported correlations. No query outcomes were available or used.

The main remaining uncertainties are sparse substitution coverage, noisily identified chemical transfer, variant-specific epistasis, possible binding/folding/readout confounding, and a training distribution partly comprising singles versus mostly double-mutant queries. The data do not establish a structural or biochemical mechanism, and independent public-subset validation may differ substantially. Determinism, exact query coverage, finite bounded scores and zero selection were checked locally before the required protected executor. Exploration files are retained for review and are not runtime dependencies.
