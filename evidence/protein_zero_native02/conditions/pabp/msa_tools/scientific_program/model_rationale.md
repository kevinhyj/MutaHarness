Checkpoint: independent-site natural homolog log-frequency ratios combined with a weak BLOSUM62 prior. Missing K164 evidence was explicitly imputed with a separate biochemical prior. All 250 predictions were saved before refinement.

One substantive refinement: the supplied profile imposes large penalties on conservative replacements (e.g. I152M, I128M, K156R). Natural evolution constrains functions beyond this growth assay, and the profile is a lexically selected subset rather than independent phenotype observations. The final predictor therefore uses a fixed 45% raw log-ratio / 55% BLOSUM62-derived compatibility mixture. The original frequencies, supplied evidence and checkpoint remain unchanged. A few sequence-mapped RRM motif penalties retain the importance of aromatic RNA stacking, avoid assuming that generic fold compatibility ensures RNA binding, and discourage disruptive motif prolines/bulky glycine substitutions. The weights are scientific heuristics, not fitted to or selected by assay outcomes.

Validation: raw alignment and profile SHA256 hashes match the manifest; WT SHA256 matches the sidecar; the first alignment record maps exactly to WT, with K164 lowercase/unpaired; the native msa_tools.score_case reproduces the supplied sidecar exactly; declared mutations, sequence changes and evidence terms agree for every ID; all candidates have the same single-chain context, with no explicit mutable partner; higher scores favor compatibility, using a single global monotonic scale; finite scores in [-1,1] cover M001–M250 exactly once. These checks establish computational consistency, not predictive accuracy.

Uncertainty: MSA coverage is complete for 240 candidates, partial for 9, and absent for 1. All missing terms are at the unaligned K164 insertion; missing evidence is never treated as a neutral observed evolutionary term. Natural homologs provide imperfect functional priors; no measured WT or candidate fitness, mutant coordinates, explicit RNA partner geometry, stability calibration, or validated epistasis is available. Double-mutant effects are additive on the latent score, with no inferred compensatory interactions. Assay-specific gains are especially uncertain.
